# MEP Co-pilot user guide

MEP Co-pilot helps an Australian mechanical services designer take an architect's model through three signed gates: confirm the inputs, run encoded rules, and have the results checked and approved by other people.

Read this first: every rule in the app is a DRAFT. No engineer has approved any of them, so nothing here is a compliance result. See "Limits and what draft rules mean".

## Pages in this guide

- Getting started: sign in, roles, projects and revisions.
- Gate 1, confirm inputs: upload a model and confirm every value the rules will use.
- Running rules and reading results: what the run needs, what PASS, FAIL and NEEDS_JUDGEMENT mean.
- Revisions and diffs: freezing, new architect revisions, what changed and what is stale.
- Review and sign-off: Gates 2 and 3, the registration number, the independence notice.
- Share link for certifiers: a read-only, expiring link.
- Drafting skills: building IFC, DXF and fabrication files from a spec card, firm templates, the 3D preview, duct sizing.
- Services, clashes and quantities: ceiling void check, clash-lite, quantity export.
- Fix hypotheses and Performance Solutions: suggestions to verify, and evidence for a Performance Solution.
- Commissioning and the NSW declaration draft.
- Limits and what draft rules mean.
- Glossary.

## Three ideas to hold onto

- No AI decides compliance. Results come only from rule files evaluated by a fixed engine. Assistants may write notes and draft spec cards; they cannot produce a PASS or FAIL and cannot approve anything.
- Nothing read from a file is trusted until a person confirms it at Gate 1.
- The server decides what you may do. The page only shows the buttons; if you are not allowed, the server refuses and says why.
